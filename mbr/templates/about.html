About

Benchmarks measure what language models can do. This site collects what people notice about how they behave: their tendencies, quirks and personality, as reported in the wild on X.

A report can be a precise episode ("it hallucinated a message I never wrote, then solved it") or a general feeling ("it's really good at pushing back when I'm wrong"). What makes one interesting is usually a surprise: the model doing something unprompted, out of place, funny, or revealing about how it sees itself.

How it works

  1. Every hour, new tweets from the Community Archive, an open, opt-in archive of X, are scanned for mentions of about fifteen current models.
  2. Each matching tweet is read together with the tweet it replies to, the tweet it quotes, its top replies and its screenshots. {{ classifier }} decides whether it describes a behavior (not a capability, benchmark or piece of news) and scores how interesting it is from 1 to 10. Reports scoring {{ min_score }} or more appear on the site.
  3. Reports are ranked by {{ rank_quote }} × quotes + {{ rank_rt }} × log2(1 + min(retweets, {{ rank_cap }})) + {{ rank_score }} × interestingness, where quotes counts distinct Community Archive accounts that quote-tweeted the report, the best sign of a report people actually discussed.
  4. Once a day, {{ summarizer }} reads each model's reports and writes the one-line trait and short portrait shown on its card.
  5. New releases on OpenRouter are checked daily and added automatically.

Limitations

  • Coverage is limited to accounts in the Community Archive and what its stream captures, so it is a slice of X, not all of it.
  • Models are found by keywords, and reports are graded by a language model; both make mistakes. Add ?debug=1 to any page to see each tweet's score.
  • Summaries describe what people report, which is shaped by who posts and what goes viral. They are not measurements.

Data

Everything is available as plain text for language models: llms.txt (index and summaries), one Markdown file per model under /data/, and llms-full.txt with every report and its context.
